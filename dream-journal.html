A bilingual mobile app that helps people capture a dream before it fades, and understand it: symbols, emotion and meaning.

Role
Solo: research, UX, UI, build
Platform
Mobile, Hebrew and English
Type
Self-initiated
Year
2025–26

Hold the dream, then help make sense of it.

You speak or type a dream. The app breaks it into symbols, emotions and personal associations, offers an interpretation and a generated image, and tracks patterns over time. I designed and built it end to end, alone.

The problem

The minutes after waking are fragile, and the dream goes with them. After a hard experience, it can linger as a nightmare instead.

A notes app captures the text but gives nothing back. Clinical tools feel heavy. There was no gentle, private space that both keeps the dream and helps you understand it.

Who it's for

Anyone who dreams, with particular care for people whose sleep has been shaped by trauma.

Research showed dreams change with age. Children dream in direct images, monsters and pursuers. Teenagers tend toward realistic, recurring scenes of escape or freezing. Adults dream more symbolically: loss, collapse, confusion. The app has to read all three.

Meaning is general and personal at once

I grounded the analysis in three things:

  • Dream criteria. Each dream is read by type, intensity, frequency, realism and how it feels after waking.
  • A symbol map. Drawing on Jungian readings: water as emotion, falling as loss of control, a chase as pressure, a house as the self.
  • The dreamer's context. A symbol means something different to each person, so every reading combines a general meaning with the user's own associations.

All of it was tested against real, anonymized dream accounts.

The concept: a loop, not a log

  1. CaptureVoice or text
  2. Break downSymbols, emotion, association
  3. InterpretGeneral and personal
  4. See itA generated image
  5. TrackPatterns over time

The tracking step is what separates it from a journal: recurring symbols and emotions surface on their own.

The design: nocturnal, soft, readable at 6 a.m.

A night-sky system of navy and violet gradients with frosted glass surfaces. Emotions are tagged in color so patterns are visible at a glance. The app is right-to-left first, with Hebrew as the primary language, and the onboarding copy is written to feel calm rather than clinical.

Building it

I took the app from Figma to working code myself. A full design-token system and a written prompt guide let me move designs into code with exact values, not approximations. The analysis engine is a TypeScript service that uses Claude through the Anthropic SDK, with tool use to return structured output: symbols, emotions and interpretation as data the UI can render.

Designing for AI from the inside changed how I design for it. Structure the output, and the interface can be calm.
