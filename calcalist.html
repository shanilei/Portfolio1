Research

We built our foundation on thorough research: interviewing Calcalist's VP of Marketing, analyzing competitors, and examining similar applications. This approach gave us clear insights to develop an effective, data-driven strategy.

Market competitor research

We analyzed Calcalist's main competitors in the financial media market to identify differentiation opportunities and competitive advantages. Globes newspaper and TheMarker.

Competitors' Weaknesses

  • Excessive advertisements across sites
  • Overcrowded content on homepages
  • Suboptimal mobile display adaptation

Competitors' Strengths

  • Use of unique color palettes that convey credibility (blue-white in Globes, green-black-white in TheMarker)
  • Bold headlines and clear fonts that improve readability
  • Well-integrated infographics within content

Conference applications research

We explored similar apps to learn what works, analyze UX and key features, and gain insights to shape a clear, standout direction for our product. GeekTime, CalcalistC and Meetup.

Shared Areas for Improvement

  • Annoying ads – Large or pop-up advertisements disrupt the browsing experience.
  • Lack of Distinction or Organization of Categories – Difficulty in quickly identifying where the user is located or understanding the content structure.
  • Technical Performance – Slow down loading times.

Shared Strengths

  • Smart use of images – Calcalist uses infographics. GeekTime uses bold, colorful images.
  • Diverse Content – Each site focuses on its specialty domain, blending news, opinions, in-depth articles, and more.

In-Depth Interviews

We conducted in-depth interviews with frequent attendees of Calcalist events, aiming to expand on the survey findings. The research helped us better understand their needs, habits, and expectations from the digital experience, and to shape a clearer direction for developing the app.

Motivation at events

To identify emerging trends early, meet potential partners, strengthen personal branding, and build smart connections.

Pain points

  • Disorganized and unclear schedule – overlaps between sessions, difficulty locating key people to meet.
  • Difficulty filtering participants – it's not clear who is attending and what their interests are.
  • Lack of accurate information about session content – unclear if a talk is worth attending.

Needs

  • Ability to identify and meet relevant participants both in advance and on-site. Smart documentation of connections and insights from the event.
  • Advance information about session content – including summaries, speakers, and tags.
Yoav Ben-David, 47, Tel Aviv CEO of a Real Estate Development Company

Motivation at events

To attend only content that delivers real value — lectures that enhance business and precise meetings for networking or collaboration

Pain points

  • Wasted time – hates irrelevant content or lack of planning.
  • Difficulty making real-time decisions – insufficient advance info about content and speakers.

Needs

  • Advance planning of an efficient, precise schedule based on business interests.
  • Ability to schedule meetings in advance with relevant participants.

Key UX Conclusions

  • Visual Clarity – A clean, minimal interface communicates professionalism and supports better decisions.
  • Mobile-First Experience – The app must function smoothly on-the-go. Real-time usability is critical.
  • Quick content discovery – The content should be clear and well-organized.
  • Personal Relevance – Users want to instantly see “what’s for me” vs. what’s general.
  • Stay Goal-Oriented – Every feature should support building a personal schedule. No distractions.
  • Smart Navigation – Icons, colors, and visual that help users stay oriented quickly.
  • Time Awareness – Dynamic time indicators (what’s next, what’s overlapping, how long left).
